Quick valency guide

Valency is the combining capacity of an atom. For many main-group elements, the valency can be understood from the number of electrons an atom loses, gains, or shares to complete its outer shell.

Group 1 → 1
Group 2 → 2
Group 13 → 3
Group 14 → 4 (common)
Group 15 → 3 (common)
Group 16 → 2 (common)
Group 17 → 1 (common)
Group 18 → 0

Remember these variable valencies

Iron (Fe): 2, 3   Copper (Cu): 1, 2   Mercury (Hg): 1, 2

Tin (Sn): 2, 4   Lead (Pb): 2, 4   Chromium (Cr): 2, 3, 6

Important: Valency can depend on the compound. These are common school-level values for revision, not every oxidation state an element can show.

Formula-writing reminder

For a simple ionic compound, write the symbols, note their valencies, then criss-cross the numbers as subscripts and simplify if possible.

Example: Magnesium (2) + Chlorine (1) → MgCl₂

Example: Aluminium (3) + Oxygen (2) → Al₂O₃
